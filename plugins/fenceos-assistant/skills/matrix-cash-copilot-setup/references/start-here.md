# Conversation, dates and readiness

## Opening

Use an ordinary conversation, not a questionnaire. Example, adapt to what is known:

"We'll build next year's budget so you can see the sales you need, what your crews and other costs will use, and the profit you are planning for. We'll also check when money is expected to come in and go out. The target is 8 to 10 focused hours over a few sessions. I'll explain decisions, handle the browser work, and check what saves. You can start with bookkeeping reports, spreadsheets or statements; reports do not need to arrive by email. We'll identify missing items early."

Then recommend the next calendar or fiscal year and confirm it, rather than asking an unexplained year question. For example, in late 2026 a calendar-year business normally plans 2027. This is an example, not a permanent year. Respect the owner's already-selected period. Confirm the fiscal boundary if unknown before mapping months. Confirm the exact business and ask where records currently live. Do not ask for information already supplied.

Record the agreed required deliverables at intake: annual Matrix budget and its exact period, supporting Cash Copilot coverage, supported export and private checkpoint. If the owner narrows scope, mark excluded work Not requested, not complete. Explain the final outputs briefly: Matrix's annual revenue/cost/profit budget; a supporting dated Cash Copilot forecast; a saved explanation of assumptions, evidence, gaps and the weekly/monthly routine. Profit is not bank balance or guaranteed owner take-home. Cash planning does not move money.

## Start from the owner's level

A beginner needs a one-sentence explanation at each decision, not a finance lesson up front. For example: "Overhead is the cost of keeping the company running even when no fence is being installed." Offer a supported interpretation and its effect, then ask for the business decision. An experienced owner can approve the concise table without the explanation. Never infer financial knowledge from job title. Demonstrate one useful result early, such as an organized cost list or a sourced revenue baseline.

## Sources and minimum inputs

| Goal | Useful starting input | If missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual budget | Existing plan or usable income/expense report, known crew/role costs and goals | Build an explicitly provisional plan from confirmed costs and approved assumptions; do not invent history |
| Seasonality and monthly goals | Completed monthly history, preferably multiple years when available | Ask for an approved seasonal plan; label it modeled, not actual |
| Dependable cash forecast | Dated spendable cash, actual unpaid obligations, expected collections and payment dates | Keep cash readiness partial and list the specific gaps; continue independent annual planning |

Help retrieve authorized software exports, use files, or prepare the bookkeeper request. Ask which period is useful before exporting all history. Statements show money movements but do not establish all profit, unpaid bills or future payroll. Missing reports are not zero expenses. Do not rebuild the bookkeeping system or payroll service as part of setup.

Email is optional and useful even without scheduled reports: sent attachments and prior accountant exchanges may contain records. Ask for named mailbox/business/record/date scope. If email is declined or irrelevant, proceed through another source and do not repeatedly suggest connection.

## Keep dates separate

Record source-report periods, the selected annual-plan start/end, cash balance as-of date and forecast start/end independently. Urgent current cash preparation may come first, but return to the agreed next-year annual budget. A future-year plan is not permission to overwrite today's operational cash goals. A rolling 13/26/52-week view is not a complete calendar year. Confirm midweek cleared transactions before anchoring cash at a week boundary.

For an existing plan, use a verified separate-budget-copy flow if the owner wants another year; preserve the original. A selector alone does not prove separation. For a non-calendar fiscal year, verify Matrix can represent the exact months without relabeling; otherwise prepare a clearly dated private worksheet and mark product entry blocked. Do not call that worksheet a completed saved Matrix budget.

## Access without jargon

The usual sign-in is https://matrix.thefencecompany.com/login, shared by Matrix and Cash Copilot. Open it yourself after the public-guide refresh; honor a verified custom deployment. Ask for an invitation/link only if the known destination fails or the correct business is absent. Missing accounts go to support while preparation continues. Do not invent provisioning steps. A product Google login does not connect Gmail. Tool access and sign-in do not grant blanket access to all records.

A skill supplies instructions, not tools or permission. If browser control is unavailable, explain that immediately and deliver preparation work; do not spend the session trying imaginary controls.

## Reuse answers already supplied

Before the first question, inventory facts explicitly supplied by the owner, including answers in an attached owner decision card. Restate confirmed business/period briefly without asking them to answer again. Ask only what is missing, conflicting or changed. The three-question limit is a ceiling, not a requirement to ask three. If no decision is missing, take the next authorized useful step.
